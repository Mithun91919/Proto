A

Principles

One job per visual layer

A portfolio system should explain the work before it decorates the page.

The visual language is deliberately split into four responsibilities. When two layers try to perform the same job, the composition gets noisy.

Aa

Typography

Tells the story

Carries the argument, hierarchy, voice, and reading rhythm. It should remain readable without any styling trick.

Colour

Directs attention

Highlights the outcome, active idea, link, or selected state. One emphasis per headline.

Dots

Explain the system

Show quantity, grouping, connection, state, and change. Never use them simply because the site is dot-themed.

Glass

Creates depth

Groups supporting content and interaction. Product interfaces and reading-heavy surfaces stay solid.

Type / story+Colour / focus+Dots / structure+Glass / depth=clear case study

B3 · Surface hierarchy

Glass is a depth cue, not the default background. Use it when a card is meant to float above the dot field; keep content that demands precision on a solid surface.

Rule · never glass on glass · one depth level per composition · product UI stays solid

Glass / soft

Supporting context

Use for small facts, secondary evidence, chapter notes, and passive project fingerprints. Low shadow; the page grid should remain visible through it.

Glass / base

Evidence and comparison

Default card surface for metrics, decisions, comparison modules, and grouped narrative content. This is the workhorse glass level.

Glass / lift

Interactive or featured

Reserve for clickable project cards, next-project navigation, and hoverable transformations. Stronger shadow is allowed because interaction creates the extra depth.

Solid / media

Precision content stays solid

Use for reconstructed interfaces, forms, code, dense tables, and reading-heavy content. Dark anchor blocks also remain solid. Glass here would reduce contrast without adding meaning.

B4 · Divider hierarchy

Dots now mark the start of a boundary instead of forming a full dotted fence. This keeps the motif visible without cutting every section into strips.

Major divider

Five accent dots + a fading hairline. Use only between top-level chapters and guide sections.

Minor divider

Three muted dots + a short hairline. Use between component examples, evidence rows, or related narrative blocks.

Do not use a divider when spacing already makes the relationship clear. Metadata continues to use a single middle dot · rather than a rule.

B5b · Responsive behaviour

Mobile is a change in reading order, not a scaled desktop. Preserve the story sequence even when the composition collapses.

Sticky narrative

Stacks into title then body. No sticky behaviour below 820px.

Alternating rows

Always become text then media, regardless of the desktop left/right order.

Proof strips

Stack vertically and keep the metric label attached to its number.

Dot diagrams

Simplify the relationship or stack nodes. Never shrink labels below the mono token.

Fingerprints + reveals

Show the resolved fingerprint by default on touch layouts. Dot-to-interface transitions become a static split or the final interface state.

Media sequences

Four-up becomes two-up, then one-up. Preserve frame numbering.

B7 · Ambient glass

Glass needs something subtle to refract. A faint ambient cyan field may sit behind a featured card, but it should never become a page-wide gradient.

Featured glass

Use ambient colour behind project cards, hero evidence, or next-project navigation.

The glow stays outside the content layer. Text and UI surfaces keep their normal contrast.

B8 · Accessibility

Contrast

Glass, tint, and accent text must preserve readable contrast. Do not use muted colour for essential copy.

State

Colour cannot be the only state cue. Pair it with fill, shape, label, or position.

Motion

Respect reduced motion and provide a meaningful static end state.

Diagrams

Every diagram needs nearby prose or a caption that explains the relationship it represents.

D1 · Colour semantics

Colour is a reading cue, not a second illustration layer. Use accent on the idea that changes, resolves, or matters most; keep everything else in ink.

Accent

Outcome, change, active idea, selected state.

Accent deep

Muted

Quiet

Accent equivalent on dark surfaces only.

D2 · Approved title emphasis

Use only one of these patterns in a title. The highlighted words should be the consequence or core idea, not a random noun.

Single word

Best for outcome-led chapter headings.

Short phrase

Two to four words. Best for transformation headlines.

Rule: highlight one idea, usually 1–4 words. Do not highlight multiple unrelated words, and do not add dots around coloured text.

L

Anti-patterns

What keeps the system from becoming a theme
×

Do not

Stack glass inside glass.

Use one depth level per composition. Inner content becomes flat or solid.

×

Do not

Highlight several words.

One title gets one accent idea. Colour should make the hierarchy clearer, not louder.

×

Do not

Use dots as confetti.

Every dot must communicate quantity, grouping, connection, state, or change.

×

Do not

Put every screen in browser chrome.

Choose frame, crop, sequence, annotation, or one full-width interface anchor based on the argument.

×

Do not

Show a metric without its base.

Include unit, time window, denominator, or enough context for the claim to stand alone.

×

Do not

Use dark blocks for variety.

Dark surfaces mark thesis, reframe, or conclusion. One or two per case study is usually enough.

Final test: if removing a visual treatment does not remove meaning, hierarchy, or useful depth, remove the treatment.
